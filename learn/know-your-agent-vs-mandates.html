Know Your Agent is not enough: identity versus permission.

Agent identity is becoming widely available. The harder question for a bank is what that identified agent is allowed to do with a specific customer's money.

Assay · Published 1 November 2026 · Not legal advice
Short answerKnow Your Agent (KYA) answers "who is this agent and whom does it act for?". A bank also needs "what has this customer allowed it to do, who approved this exception, and can we prove it?". That is the job of mandates, approvals and evidence.

What KYA does well

KYA services verify the agent, the company that runs it and the person or business it represents, often issuing a credential or trust score. A number of identity, fraud and bot-management vendors launched agent products through 2025 and 2026, so identity is fast becoming a commodity input.

What KYA does not do

  • Record the customer's limits for this account.
  • Stop a verified agent paying a new supplier without asking.
  • Produce the record a dispute team or examiner needs.
  • Give the customer an off switch at their bank.

Authentication versus authorisation

Identity is authentication: proving who is calling. A mandate is authorisation: what the caller may do, signed by the person whose money it is. Banks have always needed both, from signature cards for cheques to consent screens for open banking.

How the layers fit

LayerQuestion answered
Agent identity (KYA)Which agent, run by whom, for whom?
MandateWhat may it do on this account?
ApprovalDid the customer confirm this exception?
EvidenceCan we prove all of the above later?
