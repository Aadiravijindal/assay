What a mandate for an AI agent should contain.

A mandate is the customer's signed instruction about what their agent may do with their money. Here are the fields that make it useful to the customer, the bank and a dispute team.

Assay · Published 1 November 2026 · Not legal advice
Short answerA good agent mandate names the agent, the allowed payees, per-payment and monthly limits, the accounts, an expiry date and what happens on exceptions, and is signed by the account holder with a strong method such as a passkey. The bank's ceilings sit above it.

Example

"My agent may pay approved suppliers up to $2,000 each and $15,000 a month from Operating ••4821 until 31 March 2027. Anything new or larger, ask me."

Required fields

FieldWhy it matters
AgentTies the permission to one identified agent, not "any AI".
Payees or payee typesNew payees are the most common fraud path, so they should trigger approval.
Per-payment limitCaps the damage of a single wrong action.
Monthly limitCaps cumulative exposure.
AccountsKeeps the agent off accounts it does not need.
ExpiryForces periodic review.
Exception ruleSays what happens outside the mandate: ask, decline or require two approvers.
Signature and versionShows who agreed to which version and when.

Bank ceilings versus customer mandates

The bank sets defaults and maximums by account type. The customer signs a mandate within them. Neither can exceed what the other allows.

Common mistakes

  • Open-ended scopes like "manage my payments".
  • No expiry.
  • Allowing new payees without approval.
  • Storing the mandate somewhere the dispute team cannot retrieve with the payment.

How mandates relate to protocols

Agent protocols handle how an agent connects and requests scopes. Google's AP2 uses signed mandates for commerce; the Personal Agent Protocol draft defines read and write scopes and lists payments and signed approvals as open work. A bank still needs to enforce the customer's limits on its own side.
